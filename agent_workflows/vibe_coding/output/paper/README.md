## Effect of the selected balanced synthesis on figures

Author decision, October 22, 2026: the primary synthesis is the equal-study → equal-pair → equal-available-year pooled log ratio, with connected study/fire-component CR2 95% intervals and Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. Balance determines the estimate; clustering determines its uncertainty. Studies retain equal weight even when two publications share one dependence component. Primary pooled results are in `balanced_synthesis/balanced_CR2_results.csv` (`status = primary_synthesis`); weights, cluster membership, deletion diagnostics and session information are alongside it. Publication-cluster and observed-only/matched-interpolated results are sensitivities; temporal fits are exploratory. REML is exploratory, uses provisional annual variances, and is stored separately under `../../exploratory/output/working_covariance/`; no paper runner mode executes it.

## SI Figure 3 — Predictor-omission sensitivity

The new figure compares the full eight-predictor sets with each individual omission, joint hydrology omission, and complete wildfire- and watershed-group exclusions (12 specifications per analyte). Time since fire is separate and retained in both complete-group comparisons. Panel A shows leave-one-publication-out equal-study RMSE differences (black diamonds), plus medians and central 95% empirical ranges of paired out-of-bag bootstrap differences (colored circles/bars). Positive differences mean omission worsens prediction. These ranges summarize resampling and held-out-composition variability; they are not formal confidence intervals. Panel B shows bootstrap selection percentages; gray × cells identify deliberately unavailable predictors. Soil property denotes organic matter for DOC and clay for nitrate. Failed draws are excluded; successful draws without held-out publications enter selection summaries only. Publication-level validation can place publications on the same fire on opposite sides of a split.

PNG, vector PDF, standalone caption and panel source tables are under `figures/si_figure_3_predictor_omission/`. Detailed predictions, coefficients, paired differences, bootstrap diagnostics, reusable split/inner-fold assignments, input hashes and session information are in `predictor_omission/`. Figure 4 and the primary model outputs are unchanged. At 1,000 attempts the full-set frequencies reproduce Figure 4; full-set LOSO predictions reproduce the existing primary predictions.

Completed expanded run: 994/1,000 successful DOC refits and 1,000/1,000 nitrate refits. Eight successful DOC draws lacked test publications; paired error comparisons use 986 DOC and 1,000 nitrate draws. DOC organic matter selection ranges from 75.1% to 82.0% when available; nitrate frequencies remain at or below 6.8%. Removing the watershed group increases nested-LOSO RMSE from 0.2789 to 0.3733 for DOC (+33.9%) and from 0.8666 to 0.8733 for nitrate (+0.8%). Removing the wildfire group leaves LOSO error unchanged for both analytes. Removing soil organic matter alone raises DOC error to 0.3449; removing runoff or both hydrology metrics lowers it to 0.2351. The predefined primary model remains unchanged. Group exclusion is more damaging for watershed than wildfire predictors in 74.2% of paired DOC OOB comparisons (16.4% ties) and 6.3% of nitrate comparisons (88.1% ties). These frequencies are descriptive, not significance tests; empirical ranges cross zero. Full prediction draws and coefficients are saved as `.csv.gz` files.

Primary predictive importance uses the nested-LOSO contrasts. Bootstrap frequencies describe stability and composition sensitivity. The group definitions retain time since fire in both exclusions: watershed area/runoff/baseflow/forest/soil versus burned area/high-severity burn. The compared groups have different numbers of candidates, and analytes differ in publications and soil variables. Nitrate full-set predictions equal the intercept-only benchmark, so its small group-omission error increase does not establish predictive skill of watershed variables. `group_contribution_summary.csv`, `paired_group_contrasts.csv` and `group_selection_stability.csv` provide the direct hypothesis-oriented comparisons. The nested-LOSO and OOB comparisons hold out publications, not independent wildfire components.

## Figure 3 — Predictor-group importance relative to intercept-only

Rows are ordered intercept only, time, time + wildfire, time + watershed, and time + watershed + wildfire. Points show percent reduction in nested cross-validated RMSE from the intercept-only model within each analyte; negative values indicate worse prediction. Intercept-only appears at zero. The time row separates information from time itself from the additional contribution of predictor groups. Watershed candidates are area, runoff, baseflow index, forest cover, and organic matter (DOC) or clay content (nitrate); wildfire candidates are burned percentage and high-severity burn percentage. Time is unpenalized in all three time-plus-predictor models. Nested publication LOSO, common inner publication folds, fold-specific preprocessing, hierarchical fitting weights and one-SE tuning are retained. Evaluation gives each held-out publication equal weight. Both simple benchmarks reproduce the existing primary predictions.

These dedicated time-adjusted fits differ from the all-candidate-penalized selection and omission models. Changing the plotting baseline does not change fitted predictions.

## Paired publication errors and uncertainty

All ten pairwise comparisons among the five Figure 3 models are saved in `predictor_group_cv/paired_uncertainty/`. `paired_publication_RMSE_differences.csv` contains 190 publication comparisons. Positive `RMSE_difference` means the candidate is worse; positive `RMSE_improvement` means the candidate is better. `publication_improvement_counts.csv` reports improved/tied/worsened counts (absolute tie tolerance 1e-10 lnRR units). `overall_RMSE_improvement_uncertainty.csv` combines point estimates, counts and conditional 95% percentile intervals for absolute and percentage improvements. Overall RMSE is sqrt(mean publication MSE), not mean publication RMSE.

The full time-adjusted DOC model improves on intercept-only for six of seven publications; Uzun is worse by 0.07036 RMSE units. Time + watershed also improves six/seven; full beats time + watershed for five/seven. Nitrate time-containing models each improve on intercept-only for six/twelve and worsen for six/twelve; they tie each other in all twelve.

The 100,000 paired error-bootstrap draws sample six DOC or eleven nitrate connected publication/fire components, with Rhea/Writer always together. Publications retain equal weighting within each resampled inventory. Full DOC improvement is 0.10209 RMSE units, conditional 95% interval [0.01361,0.16014], or 27.3% [4.4%,40.9%]. Watershed DOC improvement is 0.08400 [−0.01499,0.14924], or 22.5% [−5.2%,39.5%]. Nitrate time-containing improvement is 0.07384 [−0.00882,0.15970], or 8.5% [−1.2%,18.7%]. Full versus watershed DOC improvement is 0.01809 [0.00156,0.03696]. These are conditional fixed-error resampling intervals with few components; they do not include refitting uncertainty, fully account for overlapping CV training sets, or correct shared-fire information across the original validation folds. `methods_and_limits.txt` documents interpretation; compressed draw files permit reconstruction. Figure 3 is unchanged.
